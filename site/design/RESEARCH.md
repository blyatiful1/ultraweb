# Counter-Strike History — Verified Fact Sheet

Compiled for editorial use. Every fact was checked against at least one web source (Wikipedia, HLTV, Liquipedia, Valve/Steam blogs, or reputable esports outlets). Items that could not be independently confirmed, or where sources conflicted, are marked **(UNVERIFIED)**.

## Timeline

- **June 19, 1999** — First public beta of *Counter-Strike*, a Half-Life mod created by Minh "Gooseman" Le and Jess Cliffe, released for free.
- **1999–2000 (beta era)** — Rapid beta releases (beta 1.0 through beta 7.1) on the GoldSrc engine; community mapping culture begins (de_dust, de_prodigy/nuke-precursors, de_inferno's first version, etc.).
- **2000** — Valve approaches Minh Le and Jess Cliffe about acquiring the mod; Valve hires Le and later brings the game in-house.
- **November 8, 2000** — *Counter-Strike* exits beta with version 1.0, adding official server support and polished versions of maps like Dust.
- **September 2003** — *Counter-Strike 1.6* releases alongside the launch of Steam; becomes the "gold standard" competitive version of the GoldSrc-era game, adding weapons like the FAMAS and Galil.
- **March 23, 2004** — *Counter-Strike: Condition Zero* releases (single-player-focused spin-off, plus a multiplayer mode built on the 1.6 codebase).
- **November 1, 2004** — *Counter-Strike: Source* releases, porting CS to Valve's new Source engine.
- **August 21, 2012** — *Counter-Strike: Global Offensive* (CS:GO) releases, initially a modest update to the formula before growing into the dominant esport.
- **August 14, 2013** — The "Arms Deal" update introduces weapon skins/finishes and cases to CS:GO for the first time, seeding the skins economy; player numbers reportedly grew roughly sixfold in the following months.
- **November 28–December 1, 2013** — DreamHack Winter 2013 hosts the first Valve-sponsored CS:GO Major Championship, won by Fnatic.
- **March 22, 2023** — Valve announces *Counter-Strike 2*, built on Source 2, with a limited test starting the same day.
- **September 27, 2023** — *Counter-Strike 2* fully replaces CS:GO on Steam as a free upgrade for existing owners.

**Engine lineage:** GoldSrc (CS 1999–2003/1.6, Condition Zero) → Source (CS: Source 2004, CS:GO 2012) → Source 2 (CS2, 2023).

## Maps

### Dust II (de_dust2)
**Facts**
- Original author: David "Dave" Johnston, who as a teenager also made the earlier map **de_dust** (released with the CS beta in 1999).
- Johnston has said his first Dust design was inspired by early *Team Fortress 2* concept-art screenshots he'd seen (arches, stone roads, ornate trims), which shaped the visual style he carried into Dust II.
- de_dust2 itself first released in **March 2001**, as a spiritual successor/expansion of de_dust with a more open, two-bombsite layout.
- Appeared in: CS 1.0/1.6, Condition Zero, CS: Source, CS:GO, CS2.
- **2017 CS:GO rework**: pulled from Active Duty on Feb 3, 2017 into its own map group so Valve could rebuild it; the reworked version hit the beta depot Oct 10, 2017 and then went live, keeping the layout but overhauling textures/lighting.
- **Removed from Active Duty** on Nov 18/19, 2022, replaced by Anubis ahead of the 2023 Paris Major (remained playable in Casual/Deathmatch/Competitive).
- **Returned to Active Duty** later (map pool rotations continued into the CS2 era; exact CS2 return date to the competitive Active Duty group beyond casual availability is (UNVERIFIED) as of this writing — pool churn has continued through 2024–2026).
**Callouts:** Long A, Catwalk, Short/Cross, Tunnels, Mid Doors.
**Moments/Trivia**
- Widely called the most-played competitive FPS map in history; used as the default showcase map in countless CS:GO/CS2 trailers.
- The 2017 rework was controversial among pros for changing sightlines while keeping the "classic" layout intact.
- Dust2 was removed from Active Duty in Nov 2022 for the first time since its debut, a symbolic changing-of-the-guard moment as Anubis (a community map) took its place.

### Mirage (de_mirage)
**Facts**
- Origin: Michael "BubkeZ" Hüll's CS 1.6 community map **de_cpl_strike**, made for Cyberathlete Professional League (CPL) tournaments; Hüll has stated it evolved from his earlier custom maps roughly in the mid-2000s (UNVERIFIED exact year, commonly cited as circa 2004–2006).
- de_cpl_strike/Mirage was ported into the community as "de_mirage" and saw further community CS:Source versions before Valve's involvement.
- Valve's official CS:GO remake, credited to Valve alongside community mappers, was added to CS:GO on **June 12, 2013**.
- Visual theme shifted over versions from an Italy-like look to a Moroccan/North-African aesthetic.
- Appeared in: CS 1.6 (as custom map), CS:Source (community ports), CS:GO (official, 2013), CS2.
**Callouts:** Palace, Jungle, Ramp, Connector/Con, Apartments.
**Moments/Trivia**
- Became one of the most consistently played Active Duty maps in CS:GO/CS2 history, rarely removed from the pool.
- Its A-site "Jungle" smoke/execute plays are a staple of pro VOD reviews.

### Inferno (de_inferno)
**Facts**
- Original author: Chris "Narby" Auty; the map first appeared in **1999**, in an early Counter-Strike beta version (commonly cited as beta 1.1, June 1999) (UNVERIFIED precise version number).
- Design inspiration reportedly drew from Californian mission architecture (San Juan Capistrano), evolving visually into a European-village theme over later versions.
- **2016 CS:GO rework**: Valve widened key paths (notably Banana) and the Apartments/Window Room area, and brightened the map while keeping the overall layout.
- Appeared in: CS beta/1.0/1.6, Condition Zero, CS:Source, CS:GO, CS2.
**Callouts:** Banana, Apartments, Second Mid/Second, Arch/Archway, Library.
**Moments/Trivia**
- One of the "original seven" maps to survive from the earliest CS betas all the way into CS2's Active Duty pool.
- Banana is one of the most infamous choke points in competitive CS, the subject of countless molotov/incendiary lineup guides.

### Nuke (de_nuke)
**Facts**
- Original author: Jo Bieg (his first Half-Life map was "Tokamak," April 1999); Nuke was released as part of Counter-Strike **Beta 4.0** around **November 1999**.
- The claimed alias "Zaphod" could not be confirmed as belonging to Jo Bieg — a different, separate CS level designer credited elsewhere uses the handle "Zaphod Beeblebrox" (David Marsh). **(UNVERIFIED / likely misattribution)**
- **2016 CS:GO rework**: Valve significantly reworked Nuke's geometry and rotations; the map is known for its distinctive **vertically stacked ("double-decker") bombsite A**, with a lower "Heaven"/vents area above the main A site — a rare multi-level bombsite design in competitive CS.
- Appeared in: CS beta/1.0/1.6, Condition Zero, CS:Source, CS:GO, CS2.
**Callouts:** Ramp, Heaven, Secret, Outside, Squeaky Door/Silo.
**Moments/Trivia**
- Nuke's vertical A-site design is frequently cited by analysts as one of the most tactically unique bombsites in the competitive pool.
- Long survived as one of the least-changed "classic" maps by layout, despite heavy visual reworks.

### Train (de_train)
**Facts**
- Original author: Chris "Warby"/Christopher Mair at **Barking Dog Studios**, first appearing in early Counter-Strike versions.
- **2014 CS:GO rework** (Dec 10, 2014): Valve rebuilt Train visually and adjusted gameplay while retaining the train-yard layout.
- **Removed from Active Duty** on **May 3, 2021**, replaced by Ancient (this followed Ancient's initial introduction in the Operation Broken Fang content drop of Dec 4, 2020).
- **CS2 rework returned November 13, 2024**, redesigned with a rainy night setting, a reworked/pushed-back Bombsite A, removal of "CT Heaven," and fewer train cars on A.
- Appeared in: CS 1.0/1.6, Condition Zero, CS:Source, CS:GO, CS2.
**Callouts:** Ivy/Ivy Room, Alley, Popdog, Main Platform, Z-house.
**Moments/Trivia**
- Long considered a "love it or hate it" map among pros due to its congested chokepoints.
- Its 2024 CS2 comeback drew mixed pro reaction — some pros publicly said they were unsure about the new layout.

### Overpass
**Facts**
- A **Valve-original** map, not a community port; introduced in CS:GO's **Winter Offensive update, December 2013**, alongside Cobblestone — the first map built specifically and exclusively for CS:GO.
- Set at a canal overpass/park area themed around Berlin, Germany.
- Appeared in: CS:GO (2013–), CS2.
**Callouts:** Bathrooms, Monster/Pit, Connector, Party (B site), Fountain.
**Moments/Trivia**
- The **"Olofboost"**: at DreamHack Winter 2014 (quarterfinal vs. LDLC), Fnatic's Olof "olofmeister" Kajbjer used a pixel-precise three-man boost from CT spawn onto an unintended high ledge, giving him sightlines across much of the map; LDLC lost that half 3–12. Valve subsequently patched the spot and added an in-game sign near the location.
- One of the longest-continuously-active maps in the CS:GO/CS2 Active Duty pool.

### Cache (de_cache)
**Facts**
- Origin: created by Salvatore "Volcano" Garozzo, first released as a Counter-Strike: Source community map around **2011** (a "final version" is cited as May 16, 2011).
- Garozzo (Volcano) himself ported it to CS:GO; **FMPONE began a full re-texture/remake pass starting May 23, 2013**, with Volcano's support/blessing.
- Added to official CS:GO competitive matchmaking with **Operation Breakout (2014)**.
- **2019 rework**: FMPONE announced (Oct 2019) he was rebuilding Cache on Source 2 after Valve reportedly acquired the map from its creator.
- **Removed from Active Duty March 28, 2019**, replaced by Vertigo; later returned via CS2-era map pool rotations (specific CS2 Active Duty return date is (UNVERIFIED) — reporting from mid-2026 references Cache re-entering rotation, replacing Overpass, in a "Season 5" update).
- Appeared in: CS:Source (community), CS:GO (official, 2014), CS2.
**Callouts:** Squeaky/Quad, Vents, Mid Doors, Sandbags, Boiler.
**Moments/Trivia**
- One of only two community-made maps (with Anubis) ever promoted into the official CS:GO Active Duty pool.
- Set in an industrial zone near Pripyat/Chernobyl, giving it a distinct post-Soviet aesthetic among official maps.

### Ancient
**Facts**
- A **Valve-original** map, introduced as part of Operation Broken Fang content on **December 4, 2020**.
- Promoted into the **Active Duty pool on May 3, 2021**, replacing Train — the first Active Duty change in roughly two years at that point.
- Appeared in: CS:GO (2020–), CS2.
**Callouts:** Donut, Cave, Ramp Room, Elbow, Bridge/Mid.
**Moments/Trivia**
- Set in a Central-American jungle/temple theme, unusual among the mostly urban/industrial official map roster.
- Its introduction ended Train's multi-year unbroken Active Duty tenure.

### Anubis
**Facts**
- A **community map**, created by Roald, jakuza, and jd40, winners of the **Mapcore Mapping Contest 2019** ($15,000 prize contest).
- Added to CS:GO in **April 2020** (moved from Scrimmage to Competitive matchmaking just ten days after debut, on April 10, 2020).
- Temporarily removed from the game on **May 3, 2021**; returned **August 16, 2022**, timed to CS:GO's 10th anniversary.
- Promoted to the **Active Duty pool on November 18, 2022**, replacing Dust II ahead of the 2023 Paris Major — the second community map (after Cache) ever to reach Active Duty.
- Appeared in: CS:GO (2020–), CS2.
**Callouts:** Palace, Canal, Connector, Heaven, Stairs.
**Moments/Trivia**
- Egyptian-temple theme; unusual vertical Palace structure overlooking bombsite A.
- Being only the second community-designed map to reach Active Duty (after Cache) marked a notable moment for Valve's continued openness to fan-made maps via Steam Workshop/contests.

## Era notes

- **Orange "dev texture" blockout culture**: Level designers commonly grey/orange-box out map geometry before art passes — an aesthetic and workflow that became so associated with CS mapping communities that "orange" placeholder textures are a recognizable meme/reference point in CS level-design tutorials and Workshop betas.
- **Radar/overview maps & callouts**: Each official map ships with a top-down "radar" overview; community and pro terminology ("callouts" like Long A, Banana, Heaven) emerged organically from scrims and casting, later standardized in-game via radar callout labels in CS:GO/CS2.
- **Hostage vs. defusal**: Counter-Strike originally shipped with two core objective types — hostage-rescue maps (cs_) and bomb-defusal maps (de_) — inherited from the original mod design; defusal maps (Dust2, Mirage, Inferno, Nuke, Train, Overpass, Cache, Ancient, Anubis) came to dominate the competitive map pool, while hostage maps (e.g., cs_militia, cs_office) remain more common in casual play.
- **The 2013 Arms Deal update** (Aug 14, 2013) introduced weapon skins/cases to CS:GO, effectively creating the modern skins economy and marketplace; commonly credited with a major boost in the game's player base in the following months.
- **Majors history**: The first Valve-sponsored Major was **DreamHack Winter 2013** (Nov 28–Dec 1, 2013, Jönköping, Sweden), won by Fnatic. Between 2013 and 2023 there were roughly 19 CS:GO Majors; counting CS2-era Majors brings the total into the low-20s (exact running total (UNVERIFIED) as it continues to grow).
- **CS2's Source 2 upgrades**: introduces "sub-tick" networking (recording player actions at the precise moment they occur rather than snapping to the nearest server tick) and fully volumetric, voxel-based smoke grenades that are consistent for all viewing angles and can be parted by gunfire/explosions.
