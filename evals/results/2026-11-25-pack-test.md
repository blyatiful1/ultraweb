# One design pack, two clients, the smallest model, 2026-11-25

Follow-up to `2026-11-25-art-director-split.md`. That round showed the smallest Claude tier can fill a design made by the top tier without damaging it, and that the art direction is too expensive to pay per site. This round pays for it once.

## Setup

- **The pack.** The top tier was asked for a reusable look for one-page sites of small businesses, makers and places, without knowing any client: three colourways, three first screens (a drawing, the client's own table, type as the picture), at least ten section patterns, a catalogue page that passes the gates, and a guide for the builder. It produced "Fieldbook": a page set like a surveyor's field book, narrow tall headlines, mono figures, hard rules, a numbered sheet mark beside each section. 95 KB, 17 patterns.
- **The briefs** were fixed before the pack existed: a volunteer observatory's public Friday nights, and a bladesmith who sharpens knives by post and forges a few to order.
- **Pack arm.** The smallest tier reads the pack, chooses a colourway, a first screen and seven to nine sections, fills them with the client's content, adds no CSS, then runs the finish pass.
- **Comparison arm.** The middle tier with Anthropic's `frontend-design` skill and nothing else.

One grader, blind, letters shuffled per brief.

## Result

| Brief | Grader preferred | Pack arm | Comparison arm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observatory | comparison arm | $0.35, 6 min 03 s, gates pass | $0.64, 3 min 43 s, gates fail (3 checks) |
| Bladesmith | pack arm | $0.28, 4 min 49 s, gates pass | $0.64, 3 min 46 s, gates fail (7 checks) |

The pack itself cost $4.57 and 15 min 19 s, once. Costs are API list prices, priced per request, in a session that adds about 100K tokens of harness context to every agent.

- Per site the pack arm cost about half of the comparison arm ($0.31 against $0.64 on average), and its pages passed the gates both times, the new text-overlap check included.
- At that difference the pack is paid back after about 14 sites, provided the pages are as good. On looks it was one of two.
- Both builders left the pack's stylesheet untouched and added none of their own. One page carries two inline style attributes, which the instructions forbid.
- The two pack pages do not look alike: the builder chose the dark colourway and the timetable first screen for the observatory, the light colourway and the drawing first screen for the bladesmith.

## Reading the split decision

This part is interpretation, from looking at the four pages.

- The bladesmith page won with a dimensioned side elevation of a knife, numbered callouts and a parts list. That is the pack's own idiom meeting a subject made of measurements.
- The observatory page lost to a page with a drawn dome under stars, a status line ("No public night today") and a red-light mode for dark-adapted eyes. The pack page was a correct timetable on navy: no night, no wonder, half the first screen empty. A field-book look does not carry a subject whose appeal is atmosphere, and the builder chose the timetable where a drawing of the dome was available.
- So one pack is not a general answer. A pack wins where its character fits the client. That is an argument for several packs with different characters and a choosing step, not against packs.

## Faults found

- The pack's headline style hyphenates words ("sharp-ened", "de-grees").
- The pack's guide lets the builder pick a first screen that leaves the subject's most obvious picture unused; it should say when a drawing is the better choice.
- The pack cost nearly twice the estimate made before the run.

## Limits

One pack, two briefs, one build per cell, one grader. The pack, its authoring instructions and the builder instructions are prototypes and are not in the repository.
